UNTITLED CITY TRADE GAME
Detailed Game Concept — GPT Revision 4
======================================

STATUS
------
This document is the fourth and final concept revision before numerical balancing.

The design is now intentionally frozen at the concept/rules level.
All remaining core rule contradictions from the previous reviews are resolved.
The next major document should be a NUMBERS SHEET for the paper prototype.

The public/commercial title is NOT decided yet.

============================================================
1. CORE IDEA
============================================================

Untitled City Trade Game is a competitive social strategy game for 4 players.

Each player controls a different city in the same regional economy.
Every city is naturally strong at producing one important resource, but no city
can efficiently produce everything it needs.

As a result, players are rivals who are also economically dependent on one another.

The emotional core is:

"I need something from you, but you are also my rival."

Players:
- build and develop their cities
- trade resources
- negotiate
- make promises
- sign contracts
- react to future crises
- compete for public projects
- pursue hidden objectives
- try to finish with the highest Prestige

The game should create memorable situations such as:
- "You saved me during the Energy shortage."
- "I trusted your promise and you broke it."
- "Everybody thought I was losing, but my hidden objectives changed the result."
- "We worked together on the public project and then competed against each other."
- "I controlled the resource everyone needed during the drought."

3. VERSION 1 SCOPE
============================================================

Version 1 should stay deliberately small.

The objective is not to create a large commercial game immediately.
The objective is to answer:

"Is the core interaction fun enough that people want to play again?"

Version 1 contains:
- 4 players
- 4 asymmetric cities
- 5 resources
- exactly 14 rounds
- simultaneous play
- one combined Development & Trade Window
- global market with buy/sell spread
- instant trades
- verbal promises
- one simple formal contract system
- 10–12 world events
- 3 city levels
- 1–2 public projects per match
- hidden objectives
- regional bidding opportunities
- Prestige scoring
- no player elimination

Version 1 does NOT require:
- armies
- territory conquest
- sabotage
- teams
- solo campaign
- sophisticated AI negotiation
- matchmaking
- asynchronous play
- loans
- futures
- embargoes
- trade taxes
- 6 players
- complex diplomacy
- mobile monetization
- DLC design
- a large map


============================================================
4. MATCH END
============================================================

Version 1 uses a FIXED GAME LENGTH.

The game lasts exactly:

14 rounds.

There is no early Prestige trigger in the first prototype.

After Round 14:
1. All normal round effects finish.
2. Hidden objectives are revealed.
3. Hidden Prestige is added.
4. Final Prestige is calculated.
5. The player with the highest total Prestige wins.

This fixed structure has several advantages:
- every test game has comparable length
- balancing is easier
- there is no confusion between visible and hidden Prestige
- players know exactly how much time remains
- the game cannot continue indefinitely

A future version may test an early-win trigger after real score distributions
are known.


============================================================
5. THE FOUR CITIES
============================================================

The four Version 1 cities are:

1. AGRICULTURAL CITY
Strong at producing Food.

2. INDUSTRIAL CITY
Strong at producing Materials.

3. ENERGY CITY
Strong at producing Energy.

4. TECHNOLOGY CITY
Strong at producing Technology.

Every city can earn Money.
Every city can acquire all resources.
Every city can produce outside its specialty, but less efficiently.

The exact production numbers belong in the future Numbers Sheet.

A Trading City is NOT part of Version 1.


============================================================
6. RESOURCES
============================================================

Version 1 has exactly five resources:

FOOD
Used for growth, development, events, and some projects.

ENERGY
Used for infrastructure, development, events, and some projects.

MATERIALS
Used for construction, development, buildings, and infrastructure.

TECHNOLOGY
A normal tradable resource.
Used from the EARLY GAME onward.

Technology is spent on:
- city upgrades
- buildings
- project contributions
- crisis responses
- regional opportunities

Technology is NOT a separate progression track in Version 1.

MONEY
Used for:
- market transactions
- bids
- some buildings
- public projects
- contracts
- crisis responses

There is no separate:
- Medicine
- Population
- Influence

system in Version 1.


============================================================
7. RESOURCE DEMAND PRINCIPLE
============================================================

All four specialty resources must have meaningful demand from the beginning
of the match.

The design must avoid this problem:

Food: high demand
Energy: high demand
Materials: medium demand
Technology: low demand

That would make the Technology City weak.

Therefore the Numbers Sheet must contain a DEMAND TABLE.

The table must list every cost in the game:
- city upgrades
- buildings
- upkeep
- events
- public projects
- opportunities
- objective-related actions

For each cost, record which resources it consumes.

The design target:
Food, Energy, Materials, and Technology should all have meaningful uses in
the early, middle, and late game.

The resources do NOT need identical demand, but none should become irrelevant
for several rounds.


============================================================
8. STORAGE
============================================================

Food, Energy, Materials, and Technology have limited storage.

Initial concept:
Food: capacity 10
Energy: capacity 10
Materials: capacity 10
Technology: capacity 10
Money: unlimited for the first prototype

Some upgrades may increase storage.

STORAGE TIMING RULE

Storage capacity is enforced at ROUND RESOLUTION, not continuously.

During the Development & Trade Window, a player may temporarily hold more
than the normal storage limit.

Example:

Food capacity:
10

Current Food:
9

The player receives:
4 Food through trade.

Temporary Food:
13

This is allowed during the active window.

Before Round Resolution finishes, the player must reduce Food back to 10
or lower by:

- spending it
- trading it
- selling it to the global market

Any excess still above storage capacity at Round Resolution is discarded.

Why use end-of-round enforcement?

- trades are not blocked by temporary overflow
- players can reorganize resources freely
- preparation for forecast crises is more flexible
- storage still matters because excess cannot be carried into the next round

Money being unlimited is something to WATCH during playtesting.

If players constantly convert all surplus resources into Money, then:
- market buy prices may be too generous
- Money may need its own storage/maintenance rule later

Do not add that complexity before testing.


============================================================
9. CITY DEVELOPMENT
============================================================

Version 1 has three city levels.

LEVEL 1 — SETTLEMENT
Starting level.

LEVEL 2 — CITY
Unlocks:
- better production
- more building choices
- stronger specialization
- some project advantages

LEVEL 3 — METROPOLIS
Unlocks:
- advanced production
- advanced buildings
- stronger specialization
- additional Prestige opportunities
- stronger crisis-response options

Three levels are enough for a 14-round game.


============================================================
10. UPKEEP
============================================================

The previous Food + Energy upkeep rule is removed.

It unfairly benefited the Agricultural and Energy cities.

Version 1 uses NON-SPECIALTY UPKEEP.

A city may NOT use its own specialty resource to pay upkeep.

Specialty restrictions:

Agricultural City:
cannot use Food for upkeep.

Industrial City:
cannot use Materials for upkeep.

Energy City:
cannot use Energy for upkeep.

Technology City:
cannot use Technology for upkeep.

LEVEL 1
No upkeep.

LEVEL 2
Pay 1 non-specialty, non-Money resource per round.

LEVEL 3
Pay 2 DIFFERENT non-specialty, non-Money resources per round.

Example:

Agricultural City at Level 3 may pay:
1 Energy + 1 Materials

or:
1 Energy + 1 Technology

or:
1 Materials + 1 Technology

It may NOT use Food for upkeep.

Money cannot be used for normal city upkeep.

Why this rule exists:

- every advanced city depends on resources produced efficiently by rivals
- all four city specializations follow the same rule
- no specialization receives a built-in upkeep advantage
- development increases economic interdependence

This rule must be watched carefully during playtesting.

If it makes trading too mandatory or creates excessive frustration, the
Numbers Sheet can reduce upkeep amounts before changing the core rule.


============================================================
11. FAILED UPKEEP
============================================================

If a player cannot pay full upkeep:
the city becomes STRAINED.

A Strained City:
- remains at its current level
- receives reduced production NEXT ROUND

Production is NOT reduced retroactively.

Exact Strained penalties belong in the Numbers Sheet.

The player does not automatically lose a city level.


============================================================
12. ROUND STRUCTURE
============================================================

Each of the 14 rounds follows the same structure.

1. AUTOMATIC UPDATE
- production resolves
- upkeep is paid
- contract obligations due now are checked
- scheduled effects resolve
- Strained penalties from the previous round apply

2. WORLD UPDATE
- current event resolves
- next major event warning is shown
- public opportunities/projects may appear

3. DEVELOPMENT & TRADE WINDOW
All players act simultaneously.

During the same window, players may:
- negotiate
- trade
- accept/counter offers
- buy/sell through the global market
- build
- upgrade
- spend Technology
- contribute to projects
- submit bids
- sign contracts
- inspect public information

Resources received through trade can be used IMMEDIATELY in the same window.

4. ROUND RESOLUTION
- bids resolve
- public project progress resolves
- Prestige earned this round is updated
- temporary effects expire
- round ends


============================================================
13. DEVELOPMENT & TRADE WINDOW
============================================================

This is the main interactive part of the round.

Initial digital target:
approximately 2–3 minutes.

Paper prototype:
may be untimed at first.

Later paper tests should introduce timers gradually.

Why one combined window?

Because this allows:

Trade:
"I need 2 Materials."

Receive:
2 Materials.

Immediately:
Use those Materials to build.

This creates immediate value from negotiation.

The older structure:
Build first → trade afterward

is removed because it forced traded resources to wait until the next round.


============================================================
14. PLAYER-TO-PLAYER TRADING
============================================================

Players may offer exchanges during the combined window.

Examples:
2 Food for 1 Energy
3 Materials for 4 Money
1 Technology + 2 Money for 2 Energy

Players can:
- accept
- reject
- counteroffer

All negotiations happen simultaneously.

There is no individual trading turn.


============================================================
15. THREE TYPES OF AGREEMENT
============================================================

A. INSTANT TRADE

Example:
2 Food for 1 Energy.

If both sides accept:
the game executes it immediately.

Instant trades are binding.


B. VERBAL PROMISE

Example:
"Give me Energy now and I will help you next round."

The game does NOT enforce this.

It is socially non-binding.

The player may:
- keep the promise
- renegotiate
- break the promise

This creates bluffing and social stories.


C. FORMAL CONTRACT

A formal contract is explicitly recorded by the game.

Example:

Round 6:
Player A gives 2 Energy now.

Round 7:
Player B owes 3 Food.

Formal contracts are normally binding obligations.

However, they can fail or be intentionally broken.


============================================================
16. FORMAL CONTRACT RULES
============================================================

Version 1 contract rules:

MAXIMUM DURATION
3 rounds.

GAME-END RULE
No formal contract obligation may be due after Round 14.

Therefore:

- a contract created in Round 12 may have an obligation due in Round 13 or 14
- a contract created in Round 13 may have an obligation due in Round 14
- a contract created in Round 14 cannot create a future obligation

Instant trades are still allowed in Round 14.

This prevents players from receiving resources near the end of the game in
exchange for promises that can never come due.

CONTRACT FAILURE
If a player cannot satisfy an obligation when it becomes due:
the contract is automatically broken.

VOLUNTARY BREAK
A player may intentionally break the contract.

COMPENSATION
Contract-break compensation is denominated primarily in Money.

The compensation goes to the VICTIM, not to the bank.

If the breaking player cannot pay the full required Money compensation:

- the available Money is paid to the victim
- the unpaid portion creates an additional Prestige penalty for the breaker
- Prestige is NOT transferred to the victim

This avoids creating a kingmaking tool while still making default costly.

PUBLIC RECORD
The breaking player receives:
Contracts Broken +1

This number is public.

PRESTIGE
Contract breaking may cause a Prestige penalty.

MUTUAL CANCELLATION
Both players may agree to cancel a contract without penalty before the
obligation becomes due.

The exact Money compensation and Prestige penalty values belong in the
Numbers Sheet.


============================================================
17. SIMPLE REPUTATION
============================================================

Version 1 does not need a complicated reputation system.

Public information includes:

CONTRACTS BROKEN: X

Players decide for themselves whether another player is trustworthy.

The game does not assign labels such as:
"Honest"
"Dishonest"


============================================================
18. GLOBAL MARKET
============================================================

The global market is a fallback.

It prevents complete economic lockout.

It should NOT be the best way to trade.

Each resource has:

MARKET SELL PRICE
What the player pays to buy from the market.

MARKET BUY PRICE
What the market pays when the player sells.

There must be a spread.

Example:

Market sells:
1 Energy for 3 Money.

Market buys:
1 Energy for 1 Money.

Human trade:
1 Energy for 2 Money.

Then:
buyer prefers player trade

and

seller prefers player trade.

This protects the core social mechanic.


============================================================
19. MARKET BEHAVIOR
============================================================

For the first prototype:
market prices should remain simple.

MARKET PRICE TIMING

Market prices are FIXED during the entire Development & Trade Window.

They do NOT change after each individual purchase or sale.

At Round Resolution:

- total market buying for each resource is counted
- total market selling for each resource is counted
- event modifiers are applied
- next round's prices are calculated

The updated prices become active at the start of the next round.

This prevents click order from determining price and makes the rule easy to
manage in a paper prototype.

Prices may move within a narrow predefined range based on:
- net demand
- net supply
- events

The system should not become a complex financial simulator.

During tests, measure:
- number of market transactions
- number of player-to-player trades

If global market usage dominates human trading:
the market is too attractive.


============================================================
20. WORLD EVENTS
============================================================

Version 1 should contain approximately:

10–12 handcrafted event cards.

Events should create decisions.

Events should NOT simply say:
"Randomly lose resources."

Possible events:

DROUGHT
Food production is reduced during the event round.

ENERGY SHORTAGE
Energy production is reduced or Energy costs increase.

CONSTRUCTION BOOM
Materials gain temporary extra value.

TECHNOLOGY BOOM
Technology-related costs improve.

RECESSION
Money income decreases.

TRADE DISRUPTION
Global market prices become worse.

INFRASTRUCTURE FAILURE
Players must spend Materials/Technology or take a temporary penalty.

EPIDEMIC
Uses existing resources such as Technology + Money.
No Medicine resource.

PUBLIC FESTIVAL
Players may spend Food + Money to gain a benefit or Prestige.

SUPPLY SHOCK
One resource becomes temporarily scarce.

RESEARCH BREAKTHROUGH
Creates a temporary Technology opportunity.


============================================================
21. EVENT WARNING RULE
============================================================

Most major events are shown ONE ROUND BEFORE they activate.

SETUP RULE

The first major-event warning is revealed during game setup.

Round 1 has no major crisis effect.

This gives players one learning/development round while already showing them
what they may need to prepare for.

Example:

During setup:
"Drought expected in Round 2."

Round 1:
players develop and prepare.

Round 2:
Drought effect applies.

For later rounds:

Round 5 warning:
"Drought expected next round."

Round 6:
Drought effect applies.

The event card itself describes what happens DURING the event round.

It should not say:
"Food production decreases next round."

That would accidentally add an extra delay.

The warning creates the preparation gameplay.


============================================================
22. PUBLIC PROJECTS
============================================================

A match contains:
1–2 major public projects.

Example:

REGIONAL POWER GRID

Requires contributions of:
- Energy
- Materials
- Money

Example:

REGIONAL RESEARCH INITIATIVE

Requires:
- Technology
- Materials
- Money

Projects create:
cooperation + competition.

Players want the project to succeed.

But they also want to gain more Prestige than the others.


============================================================
23. PUBLIC PROJECT RULES
============================================================

Every project card defines:
- total required resources
- duration
- minimum qualifying contribution
- success reward
- largest-contributor reward

QUALIFYING CONTRIBUTOR

A player must contribute at least the amount printed on the project card.

Example:
Minimum qualifying contribution:
3 total resources.

SCORING CONCEPT

Largest contributor:
+3 Prestige.

Other qualifying contributors:
+1 Prestige.

If largest contribution is tied:
both tied leaders receive:
+2 Prestige.

PROJECT FAILURE

If the project deadline is reached and the project is incomplete:
all contributed resources are LOST.

This increases tension and forces players to judge whether other players
will cooperate.

PLAYTEST NOTE

Because contributions are public and failure loses resources, players may
wait until the deadline before contributing.

This may create good tension or it may create frustrating stalemates.

Measure:
- whether players contribute early
- whether most contributions happen only at the deadline
- whether projects fail because everybody waits

Do not add another project mechanic until this behavior is observed.

Exact numbers belong in the Numbers Sheet.


============================================================
24. SECRET BIDS
============================================================

Some regional opportunities use secret Money bids.

Rules:

- all players may submit a bid
- 0 means PASS
- a bid must be at least 1 Money to win
- highest bid wins
- only the winner pays
- losing players pay nothing

TIE RULE

If the HIGHEST bid is tied:

- nobody wins
- nobody pays
- the opportunity remains available for the next round

Example:

Bids:
5, 5, 3, 0

Result:
nobody wins.

The player who bid 3 does NOT win.

Example:

SOLAR FIELD

Benefit:
+1 Energy production.

Players secretly bid.

Highest untied bid wins.


============================================================
25. REGIONAL OPPORTUNITIES
============================================================

The first version may use:
4–6 opportunity cards.

Examples:

SOLAR FIELD
Improves Energy production.

FERTILE VALLEY
Improves Food production.

INDUSTRIAL ZONE
Improves Materials efficiency.

RESEARCH CAMPUS
Improves Technology efficiency.

COMMERCIAL HUB
Improves Money generation.

These provide direct competition without requiring territorial warfare.


============================================================
26. HIDDEN OBJECTIVES
============================================================

At the beginning of the game:

Each player receives 3 hidden objective cards.

The player chooses:
2 to keep.

The third is discarded secretly.

Each completed objective is worth:
+2 Prestige.

Therefore maximum hidden Prestige:
4.

This is enough to create uncertainty without allowing a huge surprise swing.


============================================================
27. OBJECTIVE DESIGN RULES
============================================================

An objective should NOT directly match the player's city specialization.

Example:

Energy City should not receive:
"End a round with 8 Energy."

because it may be too easy.

Objectives should encourage intentional decisions.

Good objective concepts:

ACTIVE TRADER
Complete player-to-player trades with all three opponents.

DIVERSIFIED ECONOMY
Own at least one advanced building using three different resource types.

PROJECT PARTNER
Qualify for both public projects.

CONTRACT PLAYER
Complete two formal contracts without breaking one.

MARKET INDEPENDENCE
Complete a specific crisis without using the global market.

RAPID DEVELOPMENT
Reach Metropolis by a specified round.

The exact objective deck belongs in the Numbers Sheet.


============================================================
28. OBJECTIVE VERIFICATION
============================================================

For a digital version:
the game automatically verifies objectives.

For a paper prototype:
use one of these:

OPTION A
A fifth person acts as Banker/Game Master.

OPTION B
Use a shared spreadsheet that records hidden objective completion.

The early paper prototype should preferably have a Banker/Game Master.


============================================================
29. PRESTIGE
============================================================

Prestige is the victory score.

Most Prestige is earned through ONE-TIME achievements.

Initial concept:

Reach City Level 2:
+1 Prestige

Reach City Level 3:
+2 Prestige

Complete certain major buildings:
+1 Prestige

Complete hidden objective:
+2 Prestige

Largest public project contributor:
+3 Prestige

Other qualifying project contributor:
+1 Prestige

Tie for largest project contribution:
+2 Prestige each

Certain crisis achievements:
+1 Prestige

Special city achievements:
+1 or +2 Prestige

These are initial design values only.

The Numbers Sheet must define every exact source.


============================================================
30. WHAT DOES NOT GIVE PRESTIGE
============================================================

Simply holding resources does NOT give Prestige.

Simply holding Money does NOT give Prestige.

Reasons:
- avoids rewarding hoarding
- encourages resource use
- protects trading
- prevents score leakage from hidden reserves
- prevents Money from becoming a passive victory strategy


============================================================
31. SCORE VISIBILITY
============================================================

VISIBLE DURING THE GAME
- public Prestige
- city level
- public project Prestige
- building Prestige
- public crisis Prestige

HIDDEN UNTIL THE END
- hidden objective Prestige

Therefore players understand who appears strong but cannot know the exact
final result.


============================================================
32. NO HARD "NO FREE GIFTS" RULE IN THE PAPER PROTOTYPE
============================================================

The previous "every trade must exchange something" rule is removed as an
anti-kingmaking solution.

It was too easy to bypass.

Example:

5 Food for 1 Money

is technically a trade but can still function like a gift.

For the friends-only paper prototype:
allow uneven trades.

Observe:
- whether players abuse them
- whether kingmaking actually becomes a problem
- whether social pressure already discourages extreme feeding

Do NOT solve a theoretical problem before seeing it.

For a future public matchmaking version:
a minimum relative trade-value rule may be introduced.

This is NOT required for Version 1.


============================================================
33. NO PLAYER ELIMINATION
============================================================

Players remain in the game through Round 14.

Nobody is eliminated.

A player who is behind can still:
- trade
- complete objectives
- participate in projects
- win bids
- prepare for events
- affect the economy
- score late Prestige


============================================================
34. COMEBACK DESIGN
============================================================

The game does NOT give automatic bonuses based on being last.

Comeback opportunities come from circumstances.

Examples:
- an event increases demand for the weaker player's specialty
- hidden objectives produce late Prestige
- a project creates a new scoring opportunity
- leaders have more upkeep
- market fallback prevents complete lockout
- regional opportunities create new advantages

Players should receive opportunities, not pity bonuses.


============================================================
35. ANTI-RUNAWAY DESIGN
============================================================

The main anti-runaway systems are:

FLEXIBLE UPKEEP
Larger cities still consume resources.

STORAGE LIMITS
Strong production cannot be hoarded forever.

MARKET SPREAD
Global market conversion is inefficient.

DEPENDENCY
Every city needs resources produced efficiently by others.

ONE-TIME PRESTIGE
Leading does not automatically generate Prestige every round.

HIDDEN OBJECTIVES
The visible leader is not guaranteed to be the final leader.

FIXED 14-ROUND END
A strong early player cannot extend the game indefinitely.


============================================================
36. COMPETITION WITHOUT WAR
============================================================

Version 1 has no military system.

Competition comes from:
- resource scarcity
- negotiation
- market timing
- bids
- contracts
- city development
- public projects
- hidden objectives
- crisis preparation

Military mechanics should only be considered later if real playtests show
that the game needs more direct conflict.


============================================================
37. INFORMATION RULES
============================================================

PUBLIC INFORMATION
- city identity/specialization
- city level
- buildings
- visible Prestige
- market prices
- active public projects
- project contributions
- regional opportunity ownership
- formal contracts
- Contracts Broken count

PRIVATE INFORMATION
- exact stored resources
- exact Money balance, if testing supports this
- hidden objective details
- planned actions
- verbal promises
- negotiation intentions

This creates room for bluffing.


============================================================
38. COMMUNICATION
============================================================

The game is designed around communication.

For early testing:
players simply talk.

Possible later digital tools:
- text chat
- voice chat
- quick messages

Example quick messages:
"I need Food."
"Offering Energy."
"Need Materials."
"Deal?"
"Counteroffer."
"Too expensive."
"I can help next round."
"No deal."

Public matchmaking would eventually require:
- mute
- report
- moderation tools

These are not prototype requirements.


============================================================
39. EXAMPLE MATCH FLOW
============================================================

Four players:

GREEN
Agricultural City

RED
Industrial City

YELLOW
Energy City

BLUE
Technology City


ROUNDS 1–3

All four resources already matter.

Green has excess Food.

Red needs Food and Technology for an early building.

Yellow needs Materials.

Blue needs Energy and Materials.

Blue's Technology is useful immediately because early buildings and city
upgrades require Technology.

Trades start naturally.


ROUND 4

Warning:

"Drought expected next round."

Players react immediately during the Development & Trade Window.

Green receives several Food offers.

Red trades Materials for Food.

Blue trades Technology for Food.

Yellow offers Energy for Food.

Resources obtained from these trades can be used immediately.


ROUND 5

Drought activates.

Green has bargaining power but also needs additional resource types for
upkeep.

Green and Yellow sign a contract:

Yellow gives:
2 Energy now.

Green owes:
3 Food next round.


ROUND 6

Green receives an opportunity that also needs Food.

Green must choose:

Honor the contract

OR

break it.

If Green breaks it:
- Yellow receives compensation
- Green's Contracts Broken count increases
- Green may lose Prestige depending on final contract numbers


ROUND 8

Regional Research Initiative begins.

Blue contributes strongly.

Red contributes enough to qualify.

Yellow participates.

Green chooses not to contribute.


ROUND 10

Red reaches Metropolis.

Red has the highest visible Prestige.

Blue, however, has already completed one hidden objective.

No other player knows Blue's exact final score.


ROUND 12

Energy shortage warning appears.

Yellow becomes strategically important.

Previous relationships matter.

Yellow gives better terms to players who have traded reliably.

Yellow may demand more from Green because Green previously broke a contract.


ROUND 14

Final round.

Players make their last:
- instant trades
- builds
- bids
- project contributions
- objective attempts

Players may also settle formal contracts due in Round 14.

No new formal contract may create an obligation after Round 14.

Verbal promises about actions after the game have no rules effect.

Round resolves.


FINAL REVEAL

Hidden objectives are revealed.

Example:

Blue visible: 17
Blue hidden: 4
Blue final: 21

Red visible: 19
Red hidden: 0
Red final: 19

Green visible: 15
Green hidden: 4
Green final: 19

Yellow visible: 16
Yellow hidden: 2
Yellow final: 18

Blue wins.


============================================================
40. TIEBREAKERS
============================================================

If final Prestige is tied:

1. Higher city level.
2. More completed hidden objectives.
3. Fewer broken formal contracts.
4. Shared victory.

Money is NOT a tiebreaker.

Reason:
using Money as a tiebreaker encourages players to liquidate resources and
hoard cash.

48. CORE DIFFERENCE FROM CATAN
============================================================

Catan is inspiration for the emotional structure:

- dependency
- negotiation
- scarcity
- competition
- social pressure

This game does not reproduce Catan's rules.

CATAN
- dice-driven production
- roads
- settlements
- spatial expansion
- resource trading
- board position is central

UNTITLED CITY TRADE GAME
- asymmetric city economies
- simultaneous timed play
- combined building/trading window
- forecast crises
- global market spread
- formal contracts
- breakable promises
- flexible upkeep
- hidden objectives
- public projects
- secret bids
- fixed 14-round structure

Shared emotional idea:

"I need your resources even though I want to beat you."


============================================================
49. CORE DESIGN RULES
============================================================

1. Player interaction is more important than system complexity.
2. Every specialty resource must have meaningful demand.
3. Technology must be useful from the early game.
4. Player trading should normally beat the global market.
5. Players act simultaneously whenever possible.
6. Trading and development happen in the same window.
7. Resources received through trade can be used immediately.
8. Nobody is eliminated.
9. Version 1 lasts exactly 14 rounds.
10. Events create decisions rather than arbitrary punishment.
11. Major events are normally forecast one round early.
12. Prestige is mostly one-time.
13. Resources and Money do not directly generate Prestige.
14. Larger cities have upkeep.
15. A city cannot pay upkeep with its own specialty resource.
16. Storage limits are enforced at Round Resolution, not continuously.
17. Last place does not receive automatic bonuses.
18. Instant trades are binding.
19. Verbal promises are not binding.
20. Formal contracts can fail or be broken with compensation to the victim.
21. Formal contracts last no more than three rounds.
22. No formal contract obligation may be due after Round 14.
23. Hidden objectives are dealt 3, keep 2.
24. Hidden objectives should not directly match city specialization.
25. Money is not a final-score tiebreaker.
26. Highest bid wins; a tied highest bid produces no winner.
27. A bid of 0 means pass and cannot win.
28. Market prices remain fixed during a round and update at Round Resolution.
29. Uneven trades are allowed in the friends-only prototype.
30. Kingmaking should be measured before adding restrictions.
31. The first prototype stays small.

50. ONE-SENTENCE PITCH
============================================================

"Build a city that depends on its rivals, trade for what you cannot produce,
prepare for coming crises, and turn negotiation, contracts, and smart
development into the highest Prestige after 14 rounds."


============================================================
51. SHORT PITCH
============================================================

Your city cannot thrive alone.

Four rival cities share one economy.
Each produces something the others need.

Trade resources.

Develop your city.

Prepare for incoming crises.

Sign contracts.

Make promises.

Break them when the price is worth it.

Compete for projects and opportunities.

Pursue hidden objectives.

After 14 rounds, the city with the highest Prestige wins.

52. SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT TEST
============================================================

Before large-scale development, answer this:

"Are four people having enough fun trading, negotiating, bluffing, signing
contracts, preparing for crises, and competing for Prestige that they
immediately want another match?"

If YES:
continue developing.

If NO:
do not solve the problem by adding more systems.

Fix the core game first.
